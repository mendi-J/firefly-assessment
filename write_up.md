# Emotion Detection System for Early Identification of Emotional Distress Among University Students

---

## 1.1 Background of the Study

Mental health has become an important concern within higher education institutions across the world, especially among university students who are often exposed to academic, financial, social, and emotional pressures. The transition into university life introduces new responsibilities and challenges that can significantly affect students' emotional wellbeing. According to the World Health Organization (WHO, 2022), adolescence and early adulthood represent critical stages in mental development, and many mental health conditions begin during this period of life.

In Nigerian universities, emotional distress among students has continued to increase at an alarming rate. Several studies have reported high levels of depression, anxiety, stress, and psychological burnout among undergraduates. Orok et al. (2023) observed that depression and anxiety remain among the most common mental health challenges experienced by Nigerian students. Similarly, Ogunmodede et al. (2023) reported high levels of emotional and psychological difficulties among students in Nigerian higher institutions, highlighting the growing burden of mental health concerns within tertiary institutions.

Despite the prevalence of these emotional challenges, many students do not seek professional psychological support. Fear of stigma, social judgment, poor awareness of mental health services, and limited access to counselling facilities often discourage students from speaking openly about their emotional struggles. In many cases, emotionally distressed students continue to suffer silently until their condition begins to affect their academic performance, social relationships, and overall wellbeing.

Traditional approaches to identifying students experiencing emotional distress largely depend on self-reporting, physical observation, or voluntary counselling visits. However, these methods are often ineffective for early intervention because many students are unwilling to disclose their emotional state. This creates a significant need for alternative approaches capable of identifying emotional distress in a more discreet, passive, and proactive manner.

Recent developments in artificial intelligence, machine learning, and affective computing have opened new possibilities for emotion-aware systems. Facial Expression Recognition (FER) technologies can now analyse human facial expressions and identify emotional patterns using deep learning models. Advances in computer vision and convolutional neural networks have significantly improved the accuracy and efficiency of automated emotion detection systems (Li & Deng, 2020). These technologies provide opportunities for the development of real-time and non-invasive systems that can assist in monitoring emotional wellbeing without requiring constant self-disclosure from students.

This study therefore focuses on the development of an emotion detection system for the early identification of emotional distress among university students. The proposed system aims to utilise facial expression analysis and longitudinal emotional monitoring to identify persistent negative emotional patterns that may indicate emotional distress. By supporting early detection and intervention, the study seeks to contribute toward improved student welfare and mental health support within Nigerian universities.

## 2. Motivation for the Study

The increasing rate of emotional distress, anxiety, depression, and psychological stress among university students has become a growing concern, particularly within Nigerian universities where access to mental health support remains limited. Many students struggle with academic pressure, financial hardship, social expectations, and personal challenges, yet a large number are unwilling to seek professional help due to fear of stigma, discrimination, or lack of awareness.

Existing methods for identifying emotionally distressed students rely heavily on self-reporting and manual observation, which are often insufficient for early intervention. At the same time, recent advancements in artificial intelligence and facial emotion recognition technologies have created opportunities for developing systems capable of detecting emotional patterns automatically and in real time.

The motivation for this study therefore stems from the need to develop a low-cost, privacy-conscious, and context-aware emotion detection system that can support the early identification of emotional distress among university students. The study also aims to contribute to the development of emotion-aware mental health technologies specifically suited to the Nigerian university environment.

---

## 3. Problem Statement

Emotional distress, anxiety, and depression continue to affect a large number of university students in Nigeria. Although many students experience significant emotional and psychological difficulties, only a small proportion seek professional counselling or mental health support. Stigma, fear of judgment, limited awareness, and inadequate welfare systems remain major barriers preventing students from receiving timely assistance.

Current emotion detection systems are not adequately designed for the realities of Nigerian universities. Many existing solutions are expensive, computationally demanding, or heavily dependent on foreign datasets that may not accurately reflect African facial characteristics and emotional expressions. In addition, several existing research systems focus mainly on isolated emotion classification without incorporating long-term emotional monitoring, distress analysis, or real-time welfare support mechanisms.

As a result, there is currently a lack of practical and context-sensitive systems capable of identifying early signs of emotional distress among Nigerian university students in a passive and privacy-conscious manner.

This study therefore seeks to address this gap by developing an emotion detection system that combines real-time facial expression recognition, longitudinal emotional monitoring, and threshold-based distress analysis to support early identification and intervention for emotionally distressed students.

## 4. Aim and Objectives of the Study

### Aim

The aim of this study is to design and develop an emotion detection system capable of identifying early signs of emotional distress among university students using facial expression recognition techniques.

### Objectives of the Study

The objectives of this study are to:

1. Develop a lightweight deep learning-based emotion classification model capable of recognising human emotions from facial expressions in real time.
2. Design a monitoring system that tracks emotional patterns over time in order to identify persistent negative emotional trends.
3. Implement a threshold-based mechanism for detecting possible emotional distress among students.
4. Develop a user-friendly dashboard for monitoring emotional trends and distress indicators.
5. Evaluate the performance of the proposed system using facial expression data obtained from university students.
6. Incorporate privacy-conscious measures such as consent-based monitoring and secure data handling within the system design.

## 5. Expected Contributions of the Study

This study is expected to contribute to research and practice in several important ways.

**Technical Contribution**

The study will develop a lightweight deep learning-based facial emotion recognition model suitable for real-time deployment on resource-constrained systems.

**Academic Contribution**

The research will contribute to existing knowledge in affective computing and emotion detection by exploring the application of facial emotion recognition for emotional distress identification among university students.

**Practical Contribution**

The proposed system will provide a functional prototype that can assist university counsellors and welfare personnel in identifying students who may be experiencing emotional distress but are unwilling to seek help voluntarily.

**Contextual Contribution**

The study will provide insights into the application of emotion detection technologies within Nigerian university environments while considering cultural and ethical factors associated with mental health monitoring.

**Ethical Contribution**

The research will promote privacy-conscious and responsible deployment of emotion detection systems through the inclusion of informed consent, secure data handling, and ethical monitoring principles.

## 6. Definition of Terms

**Emotion Detection**

Emotion detection refers to the use of computational techniques and artificial intelligence methods to identify and classify human emotional states based on behavioural or physiological indicators.

**Facial Expression Recognition (FER)**

Facial Expression Recognition is a computer vision technique used to analyse facial movements and expressions in order to determine an individual's emotional state.

**Emotional Distress**

Emotional distress refers to a state of psychological discomfort associated with feelings such as anxiety, sadness, stress, frustration, or emotional instability.

**Affective Computing**

Affective computing is a field of study focused on the development of systems capable of recognising, interpreting, and responding to human emotions.

**Deep Learning**

Deep learning is a branch of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks with multiple layers to learn patterns from data.

**Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)**

A Convolutional Neural Network is a deep learning architecture commonly used for image recognition and computer vision tasks such as facial emotion recognition.

**Longitudinal Monitoring**

Longitudinal monitoring refers to the process of observing and analysing emotional patterns over an extended period of time.

**Real-Time Monitoring**

Real-time monitoring involves the immediate processing and analysis of data as it is captured.

**Welfare Dashboard**

A welfare dashboard is a digital interface designed to help counsellors or welfare personnel monitor emotional trends and distress indicators among students.

**Privacy-Conscious System**

A privacy-conscious system is a system designed to protect user confidentiality through secure data handling and minimal collection of sensitive information.
